Most gowns start as a sketch no bigger than a playing card: a neckline, a waist, the shape of the hem. It is enough to talk about, and not nearly enough to build.

## The first toile

The sketch becomes a toile, a full gown in inexpensive cotton muslin. Proportions that look perfect on paper rarely survive the first fitting, and that is the point. A muslin is cheap to cut, mark and recut.

## What changes

Necklines move by a centimetre at a time. Seams shift to follow the body rather than the drawing. By the third toile the sketch is a memory and the pattern is the design.
